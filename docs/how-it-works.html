How it works

For first timers

Turn up. Make it together.

One thing is worth saying twice, because everybody assumes the opposite: you don’t cook anything beforehand and you don’t bring a dish. You arrive with empty hands, and the whole group makes one dessert together, on the spot.

an evening with us

The four steps

  • Send us a message

    One message on Instagram, TikTok or YouTube. We reply with the place, the time and the dessert of the evening.

  • Come empty-handed

    Ingredients, bowls, a fridge: it’s all there. Whoever is hosting does the shopping, so all you have to do is find the door.

  • Make it together

    Someone walks the group through a recipe that needs no oven and no stove. Crushing, mixing and layering get shared out. It takes about an hour.

  • Eat it, keep the people

    Into the fridge, then onto the plates. You leave with a recipe you can redo without a kitchen, and with the numbers of the people you made it with.

Come for the cake. Stay for the people.

good questions

The things everybody asks

If yours isn’t here, just ask it in a message. We answer.

So I really don’t bring anything?

Really. This isn’t a potluck. Whoever hosts does the shopping, and the recipes are cheap enough that nobody collects money at the door. Turn up with your hands free.

Is it a competition?

Not at all. Nobody is graded, nobody wins, and a wobbly cheesecake gets eaten just as fast as a perfect one. We make one dessert together, as a team.

How do I sign up?

Send one message to any of our accounts; they all reach the same people. Instagram and TikTok are @cuochi_senza_fuochi, YouTube is @cuochisenzafuochi. No form, nothing to pay.

I genuinely can’t cook. Is that a problem?

It’s closer to a qualification. Every recipe is chosen so that someone who has never made anything can get it right, first try, in a group.

What does it cost?

Nothing to join. No membership, no fee, no collection at the door.

What do we make?

Mostly desserts and cakes, because they’re the easiest things to make well without heat: salame di cioccolato, tiramisù, no-bake cheesecake, chocolate mousse. All the recipes.

Do I need to bring a friend?

Most people come alone. That’s the normal way to arrive, and nobody finds it odd: the whole point is that you don’t know anyone yet.

What language is spoken?

Italian and English, often in the same sentence. If your Italian stops at “un caffè, per favore”, you’ll still have a great evening.

I have allergies, or I’m vegetarian or vegan

Tell us when you sign up. It changes what gets bought, so it really helps to know in advance. There’s almost always a version that works.

How many people come, and how often?

Usually ten to twenty: enough to meet new people, few enough to actually talk. Roughly once a month, in the evening, in a shared flat or common room in Padova.

I don’t live in Padova

Then maybe your city is next. Here’s how to start one, and we’ll help.

Is this a networking thing?

No. People do end up helping each other with flats, jobs and doctors, but the way neighbours do: as a side effect, not the agenda.

Ready for the next one?

One message is all it takes. We’ll send you the address.
