How it works

For first timers

Turn up.
Make it together.

One thing is worth saying twice, because everybody assumes the opposite: nothing gets cooked beforehand and nobody brings a dish. You arrive with empty hands, and the whole group makes something together on the spot.

The four steps

  • Say you’re coming

    One message on Instagram, TikTok or YouTube is enough. Back comes the address, the time and the recipe for that evening.

  • Turn up empty handed

    Everything is already there: ingredients, bowls, a fridge. Whoever is hosting does the shopping, so all you have to do is find the door.

  • Make it together

    Somebody walks the group through a recipe that needs no oven and no hob. Crushing, mixing, rolling and layering all get shared out. It takes about an hour and nobody stands around watching.

  • Eat the results

    Into the fridge, then onto the table. You leave with a recipe you can repeat in a room with no kitchen, and with the numbers of the people you made it with.

Practical answers

The questions everybody asks

How do I actually sign up?

Send one message to any of the three accounts, they all reach the same people. Instagram and TikTok are both @cuochi_senza_fuochi; YouTube is @cuochisenzafuochi. There is no form to fill in and nothing to pay.

So I really don’t bring anything?

Really. This is not a potluck. Whoever hosts does the shopping for the recipe, and the cost per head is small enough that nobody collects it at the door. Turn up with your hands free.

What actually gets made?

Desserts, mostly, because they are the easiest thing to make well without heat. Salame di cioccolato, tiramisù, no bake cheesecake, chocolate mousse, fruit with lemon and sugar. Sometimes a savoury thing that needs nothing but a bowl and a knife. The recipes page has the full list.

What does it cost?

Nothing to attend. There is no membership, no fee and no collection at the door.

I genuinely cannot cook. Is that a problem?

It is closer to a qualification. The name means cooks without fires, and half the club has no working kitchen. Every recipe is chosen so that somebody who has never made anything in their life can do it in a group, first try.

Do I need to bring a friend?

Most people come alone. That is the normal way to arrive and nobody will find it odd, because the whole point is that you don’t know anyone yet.

What language is spoken?

Both Italian and English, usually in the same sentence. If your Italian stops at “un caffè, per favore” you will still have a good evening.

I have allergies, or I’m vegetarian, or I’m vegan

Say so when you sign up. It changes what gets bought, so it genuinely helps to know in advance rather than on the night. There is almost always a version that works.

How many people come?

Usually between ten and twenty. Big enough that you have options, small enough that you actually talk to people rather than shout over music.

How often does it happen?

Roughly once a month, in the evening, in somebody’s flat or a shared common room in Padova. Dates go out on the social accounts a couple of weeks ahead.

Is this a networking thing?

No. People do end up helping each other find flats, jobs and doctors, but that happens the way it happens between neighbours: as a side effect, not as the agenda.

Housing is solvable. Loneliness is the expensive part.

Why this exists

You can rent a room. You cannot rent the people.

When you move somewhere new, you sort out a room and a commute within weeks. Those two things guarantee the financial capital. Human capital is the one nobody hands you a contract for, and working or studying full time leaves very little room to build it.

Cuochi senza fuochi exists because the usual alternatives don’t work for everyone. Bars cost money every single time. Clubs are loud and you meet no one. Apps put you back on a screen. Standing around a table making a dessert costs nothing, and it puts you in a room with people who live ten minutes away: the ones you can actually keep seeing.
