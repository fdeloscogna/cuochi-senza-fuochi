How it works

For first-timers

One dish. One table.
No experience required.

If you have never been to a potluck, here is the whole thing, start to finish, with nothing left to guess.

The four steps

  • Tell us you’re coming

    One message on Instagram, TikTok or YouTube is enough. We reply with the address, the time, and how many people to expect so you know how much to bring.

  • Make or buy one dish

    For roughly 4–6 people. If you can’t cook, buy something. Chocolate, bread, fruit, a bag of crisps — it all counts.

  • Show up and put it on the table

    Everything goes in the middle. Grab a plate, take a bit of everything, and start with whoever is next to you.

  • Stay for the washing up

    This is where the actual conversations happen. Nobody has ever regretted staying.

Practical answers

The questions everybody asks

How do I actually sign up?

Send one message to any of our three accounts — they all reach the same people. Instagram and TikTok are both @cuochi_senza_fuochi; YouTube is @cuochisenzafuochi. There is no form to fill in and nothing to pay.

What does it cost?

Nothing to attend. Your only expense is the ingredients for the dish you bring — in practice a few euro. There is no membership, no fee, no collection at the door.

I genuinely cannot cook. Is that a problem?

No. The name means cooks without fires — half the club has no working kitchen. See the recipes page for things that need no oven, no hob and no skill. Buying something ready-made is completely fine and very common.

Do I need to bring a friend?

Most people come alone. That is the normal way to arrive and nobody will find it odd — the whole point is that you don’t know anyone yet.

What language is spoken?

Both Italian and English, usually in the same sentence. If your Italian is at “un caffè, per favore” level, you will still have a good evening.

I have allergies / I’m vegetarian / I don’t drink

Tell us when you sign up and we’ll flag it to the group. Dishes get labelled on the table. There is always plenty without meat, and nobody tracks what you drink.

How many people come?

Usually between ten and twenty. Big enough that you have options, small enough that you actually talk to people rather than shout over music.

How often does it happen?

Roughly once a month, in the evening, in someone’s flat or a shared common room in Padova. Dates go out to the list a couple of weeks ahead.

Is this a networking thing?

No. People do end up helping each other find flats, jobs and doctors, but that happens the way it happens between neighbours — as a side effect, not the agenda.

Why we bother

Housing is solvable. Loneliness is the expensive part.

When you move somewhere new, you sort out a room and a commute within weeks. Those two things guarantee the financial capital. Human capital is the one nobody hands you a contract for — and working or studying full-time leaves very little room to build it.

Cuochi senza fuochi exists because the usual alternatives don’t work for everyone. Bars cost money every single time. Clubs are loud and you meet no one. Apps put you back on a screen. A shared table costs the price of one dish and it puts you in a room with people who live a ten-minute walk away — the ones you can actually keep seeing.
